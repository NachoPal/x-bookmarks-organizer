Reader View, and Why Bookmarking Isn't the Same as Reading

By The Sample Author · Sample Times

Half the value of a bookmarking tool disappears the moment "save for later" quietly becomes "never open again." The tab dies, the link rots, or the page behind it turns into three sidebars and a paywall banner before you get to the second sentence. The bookmark did its job - it remembered the link - but it never actually got you any closer to reading the thing.

A reader view closes that gap by doing, locally and on demand, the one thing a raw link never promises: turning a page into an actual article. Fetch it once, strip the navigation and the ad slots and the cookie banners, keep the title and the body, and cache the result so opening it a second time costs nothing. What is left afterward is exactly the two things you bookmarked it for in the first place - the words, and a way back to the source if you want the full page.

What extraction actually has to survive

Real pages are hostile to this in small, boring ways rather than dramatic ones. Inline scripts and click handlers are the obvious hazard, but the quieter failure mode is a page that is not an article at all - a redirect back to a social network, a video player with three lines of caption, a login wall. A reader view earns trust less by how well it renders a clean blog post, and more by how calmly it fails on everything that is not one: a clear message, a link to the original, never a blank panel and never a spinner that spins forever.

The measure of a reading feature is not the article it renders beautifully - it's the one it fails on gracefully.

None of this requires much cleverness. It requires doing the fetch on a server instead of in the reader's browser, giving it a real timeout, caching what comes back, and being honest with the reader when a page simply isn't readable. The rest is typography.

  • Fetch server-side, with a sane timeout and user agent.
  • Extract with a maintained readability library, not a bespoke scraper.
  • Cache the result so a re-open is instant.
  • Sanitize before rendering - it's still untrusted HTML.
  • Fail visibly, with a link out, never silently.

Do those five things and "read later" starts meaning what it says.
